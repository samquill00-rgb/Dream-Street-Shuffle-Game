# Ezekiel — Dream Street theme variation

Source: Dream Street Shuffle experiment.logicx, exported through Logic's File > Export > All MIDI Tracks as MIDI File to Music sketches/Dream Street Shuffle experiment.mid. Export contains a tempo track and four musical tracks. The lead is the first Bluebird track (21 notes); the other two Bluebird tracks contain percussion and are omitted. Upright Studio Bass is also omitted from this variation. The original Logic project was not musically edited.

This draft preserves all 21 lead pitches and their relative onset timings, at twice the source duration. Note lengths are 92% of the doubled lengths to leave breathing space. The lead begins 8 seconds into an 80-second sketch. It is a transformation of this project's melody; verify by ear that this is the theme version Sam wants. No minigame music is included.

## Tonal direction
The source melody uses G F G E, D E C, B G# A, repeated with rhythmic differences. It suggests A minor with a raised seventh near the return to A, despite the project's C-major signature. Preserve G natural in the opening and G# near A: that distinction is part of its character. Added harmony uses A–E fifths, with brief F–C and E–B fifths. No unrelated octatonic scale is imposed on Sam's theme.

## Track 1 — Organ foundation
Notes: MIDI 45 A2 + 52 E3; 41 F2 + 48 C3; 40 E2 + 47 B2. Middle C = MIDI 60; Logic may display octave names differently.
Use a low pipe-organ flute stop or a restrained organ-like sine/triangle synth. Slow attack around 1–2 seconds, release 2–3 seconds, little upper grit. No Hammond rotary effect, key click, rhythmic gate or automatic arpeggiator. A quiet 16-foot colour may be added, watching the sub-bass. Keep the deepest frequencies centred; roll low frequencies out of the reverb return. This is the vision's enormous steady floor.

## Track 2 — Dream Street theme, organ

Warm core with enough upper harmonics to stay distinct from the foundation. A dark 3–5 second hall, roughly 40–70 ms pre-delay. Keep the tune clearly audible; the bell figures must remain beneath it. Start intimate and gradually broaden the registration. No heavy vibrato. The preview is a simple harmonic synth approximation, not a sampled pipe organ.

## Track 3 — Wheels, metallic bells
Cycle one: MIDI 79 G5, 77 F5, 76 E5, one onset every 0.75 seconds, entering at 24 seconds. Cycle two: MIDI 72 C5, 71 B4, 68 G#4, 69 A4, 76 E5, every 1.25 seconds, entering at 32 seconds. Final MIDI 81 A5 at 69 seconds.
Use softened tubular bells, muted metal bars or a glass/metal hybrid. Round off the strike; avoid bright glockenspiel sparkle. Decay 2–4 seconds, gentle high-pass around 350 Hz. Very slow, shallow panning can suggest rotation; no rapid spinning stereo effect. The cycles quote pitches from the melody but are newly composed accompaniment, not original theme notes. Keep them quiet so passing clashes sound spectral rather than like wrong lead notes. Thin the layer as the last A arrives.

## Track 4 — Vision, low brass
Selective octave-below doubling of sustained lead notes in the later theme: MIDI 45 A2, 55 G3, 52 E3, 48 C3, 47 B2, then 45 A2. Quiet long horns/trombones, not staccato or epic trailer brass. Attack 0.5–1 second, tapered ending. Raise expression gently within each note; no sharp attack. Similar hall to the organ but slightly further back. Keep the brass subordinate to the melody; it enlarges the tune rather than announcing another one.

## Form and playback
0–8s: foundation alone. 8–24s: familiar melody exposed slowly. 24–40s: wheel cycles emerge. 40–64s: second thematic statement with more brass weight. 64–80s: cycles cease, final A and decay. The MIDI retains a 120 BPM timing grid; the lead behaves as a half-speed version of the original. Do not quantise away the source's small timing differences.
This is an exploration/vision arrangement demonstration with a natural ending, not a prepared seamless game loop. Reading time varies; choose repeatable beds and passage-triggered reveals after Sam's arrangement. Every minigame has separate music.
