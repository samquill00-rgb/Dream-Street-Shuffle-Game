# Nazca and Easter Island — exploration arrangements

These are original dream-score proposals, not historical reconstructions. All minigames have their own music. Fade exploration music out on minigame entry; return to appropriate exploration material afterwards. No race or combat section is included in these drafts.

Each preview lasts 72 seconds including release space. MIDI tempo is 120 BPM as a timing grid, not an instruction to add a dance beat. Four independent tracks, no plug-ins required to open. Preview instruments are simple synthesised approximations; assign the VST sounds below in your DAW. GM programs are fallback suggestions only. MIDI note numbers are authoritative (octave labels here use middle C = C4 = 60).

## Nazca — use Nazca exploration v2, replacing the earlier sketch

D major pentatonic: D E F# A B. Low D–A remains stationary; changing upper notes imply D6/9 and suspended A harmony. No drums. Let plucked patterns suggest lines on a huge empty surface.

1. Desert air — MIDI 38 D2 and 45 A2. Warm, nearly sine-wave pad with a small triangle component. Attack 2 seconds, release 2–4 seconds, low-pass around 1–2 kHz. Very slow filter movement; no obvious vibrato or pumping. Quiet beneath everything. Short diffuse room above the bass; keep deep frequencies centred.
2. Hummingbird flute — MIDI 74 D5, 78 F#5, 81 A5, 83 B5, 76 E5. Airy solo wooden flute or soft breath synth, gentle tonguing, no dramatic ornamental runs. Attack 80–200 ms, release 300–600 ms. Main phrase: D F# A B A F# E D; closing phrase D F# A B A E D. Subtle vibrato only towards the ends of longer notes. A light 1.5–2.5 second reverb, little delay. This enters around 36 seconds; it is the figure becoming visible.
3. Guitar harmonics — MIDI 62–83, mainly pentatonic upper notes. Nylon or steel-string natural harmonics; bright point of contact followed by a thin ringing decay. Do not use a thick strummed guitar patch. High-pass 200–300 Hz, gentle treble, short room and a very quiet single echo. The synthetic preview swells more than a real guitar; use a natural plucked onset in the arrangement. Closing voicing D4 A4 B4 E5.
4. Dry plucked line — MIDI 62 D4, 64 E4, 66 F#4, 69 A4, 71 B4. Close-miked fingerpicked nylon guitar, no strums, muted bass or fret squeaks. Phrase D F# A B A F# E A. Light velocity variation, mostly 35–50. Plucks about 1.25 seconds apart; leave their gaps intact. Very little reverb. Avoid heavy compression.

Timeline: 0–24 seconds scattered marks; 24–36 interlocking lines; 36–54 flute reveal; 54–72 spacious settling. The previous race section at 48–60 has been removed. This is an arrangement demonstration: do not map these elapsed times rigidly to reading speed.
Suggested eventual game assets: a subdued pampa loop, a ridge reveal cue and a quiet centre bed. Choose and test loop seams after Sam's arrangement; these supplied files have natural endings, not certified seamless loops.

## Easter Island — breath, stone and a listening presence

E minor pentatonic: E G A B D. One F natural in the horn provides a brief semitone disturbance against E. Sustained E–B supports the whole piece; there is no conventional chord progression and no combat percussion.

1. Stone fifths — MIDI 40 E2 and 47 B2. Dark breath-like synth pad, quiet bowed bass or a restrained hybrid. Attack 2–3 seconds, release 3 seconds. Keep E audible as a pitch, not sub-bass rumble. Low-pass about 1 kHz. A little grain is welcome, but avoid an aggressive cinematic braam. This represents the immobile landscape.
2. Distant breath horn — MIDI 52 E3, 59 B3 and a passing 53 F3. Soft low trombone, breathy horn or a synth combining a mellow brass core with air. Phrase sequence E, B, E, F, E, B across long separated calls. Slowly open the filter and increase upper harmonics as the call approaches. Attack 0.5–1.2 seconds; taper the release. Dark 3–5 second reverb with 40–80 ms pre-delay. The F is a quiet disturbance, not a new tonal centre. This evokes the prose's horn sound; no claim that a specific historical instrument belongs here.
3. Night harmonics — MIDI 76 E5, 79 G5, 81 A5, 83 B5. Very quiet bowed glass, bowed string harmonics or a thin spectral pad. Soft onset, 4–6 second decay, little low-mid body. Place wider than the horn, but retain mono compatibility. Slow variation rather than glittering arpeggios. These tones should be felt at the edge of hearing.
4. Thumb piano — MIDI 64 E4, 67 G4, 69 A4, 71 B4, 74 D5. A dry, rounded kalimba patch, softened tine attack, no automatic pattern or delay sequence. Widely spaced notes, velocities roughly 34–43. Slightly uneven tone is useful; extreme detuning is not. Short room or a faint long reverb send. The final E–G–B phrase is the glyph remembered. The instrument is prompted by the prose's thumb-piano image.

Timeline: 0–16 shore and isolated plucks; 16–40 distant calls; 40–56 strongest horn resonance with the passing F; 56–72 thinning into the remembered glyph. The growing intensity remains an exploration atmosphere, not a fight cue.
Suggested eventual game assets: shore/moai bed and a quieter listening/glyph bed. Reclamation uses its own music. Reading duration is variable: arrange repeatable sections and trigger the change by passage rather than an elapsed timer. Supplied files end naturally and need loop preparation before game integration.
